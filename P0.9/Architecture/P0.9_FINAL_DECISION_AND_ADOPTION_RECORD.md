# Jobbsøk_26 P0.9 — Final Decision and Adoption Record

**Project architecture:** P0.9  
**Specification revision:** 0.9.0  
**Status:** FROZEN_NORMATIVE  
**Normative relationship:** This record explains the decisions implemented by `P0.9_FINAL_NORMATIVE_SPECIFICATION.md`. If wording appears to conflict, the specification governs.

## 1. Decision method

Decisions were adjudicated proposition-by-proposition on two axes: project-decision authority and empirical authority. The Post-Critique Authoritative Directive controls every subject it addresses. P0.7 supplies implementation evidence only at its verified scope; P0.8.0 supplies unsuperseded prior norms; P0.8.1, the Opus draft and the critiques supply proposals/evidence rather than historical authority.

The adopted design follows four tests:

1. preserve every user-locked product and truth boundary;
2. delete machinery whose problem no longer exists;
3. keep strict deterministic mechanics only where they protect truth, identity, state or deliverability;
4. leave semantic quality to frontier models and human approval within factual ceilings.

`GATE-*` residue is empirical implementation verification. `EXP-*` residue is causal optimization. Neither represents an unresolved normative decision.

## 2. Product, objective and boundary decisions

### `DEC-01` — Invariant tailored CV plus tailored letter

- **Problem:** Whether the core should conditionally omit a letter for CV-only or portal-heavy jobs.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §§1–2 (`USER-LOCKED`); selective-quality objective; user permission to isolate exceptions.
- **Alternatives considered:** invariant pair; `letter_required`/nullable application; generalized required-components matrix.
- **Adopted decision:** Every core job produces both a materially tailored CV and complete application letter.
- **Rationale:** These are the stable, high-return semantic surfaces worth deep optimization. Occasional unused output has lower expected cost than branching every normal job.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some letters will not be submitted as files.
- **Supersedes:** P0.7/P0.8 conditional-letter branches; critique proposals to restore `letter_required`.
- **Specification:** §§2.1–2.2, 12.4; `ACC-01`, `ACC-12`.
- **Residue:** None. Inventory data cannot reopen this automatically.

### `DEC-02` — Portal and form generation fully downstream

- **Problem:** Whether portal questions, field limits and platform state belong in core.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §2.3–2.5 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** hardened portal-in-core; thin conditional branch; complete detachment.
- **Adopted decision:** Portal/form assistance is separate, manually initiated after an approved package and writes no authoritative core state.
- **Rationale:** Removing the surface deletes question-ID, limit, status, branching and readiness complexity rather than hardening it.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Portal-heavy cases may require downstream manual work.
- **Supersedes:** P0.8 portal contracts; P0.8.1 channel/portal hardening; Opus draft’s optional downstream tooling as a release dependency.
- **Specification:** §§2.1–2.2, 3, 17.5; `ACC-01`.
- **Residue:** A later portal tool requires a separate explicit contract, not a silent core extension.

### `DEC-03` — Minimal visible exception isolation

- **Problem:** How to prevent nonconforming approved jobs from disappearing or contaminating core.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §2.7; user decision that rare cases may be handled separately.
- **Alternatives considered:** silently drop; universalize core; first-class second workflow; minimal class plus note.
- **Adopted decision:** Every approved job is `CORE` or `EXCEPTION`; exceptions remain visible and non-blocking, with a human-readable reason only.
- **Rationale:** One small classification preserves completeness and isolation without rebuilding channel machinery under another name.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Exception handling is intentionally less automated.
- **Supersedes:** Draft exception-reason channel taxonomy and any exception workflow engine.
- **Specification:** §§2.2, 5.1–5.3, 7.5–7.6; `ACC-02`.
- **Residue:** Exact field names and human note vocabulary are implementation choices.

### `DEC-04` — Bokmål-only core

- **Problem:** Whether normal core schemas/prompts should branch by language.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §2.2 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** Norwegian/English branch; Bokmål with silent exclusion; Bokmål core plus exceptions.
- **Adopted decision:** Core language is `nb-NO`; non-Bokmål jobs are exceptions.
- **Rationale:** Language branching multiplies prompts, evaluation and templates for an irregular case outside the chosen MVP.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Non-Bokmål opportunities require separate handling.
- **Supersedes:** P0.8 multilingual control and canonical-context inventory prerequisite.
- **Specification:** §2.1; `ACC-01`.
- **Residue:** None.

### `DEC-32` — Inventory is operational; redesign trigger is human-only

- **Problem:** The draft made inventory a pre-freeze gate and used inconsistent numeric exception thresholds.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §§2.5–2.6, 3 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** `>20%`; two/three repeated reasons; no visibility; human materiality judgment.
- **Adopted decision:** Inventory supports operations and reporting only; no threshold reopens architecture automatically.
- **Rationale:** A percentage cannot override a deliberate product policy, and inconsistent magic numbers invite metric gaming.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Redesign timing depends on explicit human judgment rather than an automatic alarm.
- **Supersedes:** Draft `DEC-05`, draft `GATE-07`, canonical pre-freeze inventory interpretation.
- **Specification:** §§2.2–2.3, 18.2–18.4.
- **Residue:** Core/exception/human-scored-core/core-without-evaluable-final/submitted-known counts remain mandatory reporting.

### `DEC-31` — No runtime quality judge; durable model roles

- **Problem:** Avoid false numeric precision and volatile model-name architecture.
- **Controlling authority:** P0.8.0 review semantics, current user policy, model/surface volatility evidence.
- **Alternatives considered:** numeric fit/quality gate; LLM judge; role-based stages plus human evaluation.
- **Adopted decision:** Runtime has no model-generated quality/confidence score; stages are defined as capability roles.
- **Rationale:** Triage must predict repair action, while quality is established in human product evaluation. Commercial names change.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Human scoring is slower and less pseudo-precise.
- **Supersedes:** score-based routing and hard-coded commercial model names.
- **Specification:** §§2.3–2.5, 6.1, 18; `ACC-20`.
- **Residue:** `GATE-10`, `EXP-08`.

## 3. Run, state and lifecycle decisions

### `DEC-06` — Fresh job-centric runtime

- **Problem:** P0.7/P0.8 stage directories and movement scripts duplicated state and obscured job-level failure.
- **Controlling authority:** user permission for a fresh runtime; P0.7 completion defects; supported synthesis.
- **Alternatives considered:** retain stage-centric tree; job tree with mutable status; job tree with derived status.
- **Adopted decision:** Job is the directory/transaction unit; stages write one current artifact per job.
- **Rationale:** Failure, continuation, rerender and delivery are already job-local; aligning storage removes movement/sentinel machinery.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Status tooling must revalidate small current artifacts rather than read a progress marker.
- **Supersedes:** active P0.7 stage tree, macro/minicluster movement and `advance.ps1`.
- **Specification:** §§5.5, 6, 7.
- **Residue:** Exact tree is free.

### `DEC-07` — Current-state derivation with a canonical failure record

- **Problem:** The draft claimed fully derived failed states while forbidding status from reading history and defining no current failure input.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §8; critiques `SOL-005`/`GEM-001`; P0.8.1 validator-before-completion principle.
- **Alternatives considered:** history replay; mutable status file; current invalid artifact only; minimal current failure record.
- **Adopted decision:** Status derives from manifest, current artifacts/input hashes, current failure/blocking record and render report; history is audit-only.
- **Rationale:** This is the smallest mechanism that distinguishes never-started, failed and blocked without event sourcing.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** One additional small current record exists when failure must persist.
- **Supersedes:** draft history-event derivation and marker-based completion.
- **Specification:** §§7.1–7.4; `CTR-03`, `ACC-05`.
- **Residue:** Exact failure schema is builder freedom.

### `DEC-08` — Expected set from the frozen manifest only

- **Problem:** Directory discovery can silently omit missing jobs.
- **Controlling authority:** P0.8.1 hardening adopted into P0.9; one-authority design.
- **Alternatives considered:** scan directories; separate expected-set file; derive from manifest job classifications.
- **Adopted decision:** Every approved opportunity is in the manifest; expected core/exception sets derive from it.
- **Rationale:** A missing directory becomes a visible failure instead of disappearing.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Freeze validation must be strict.
- **Supersedes:** directory discovery and duplicate expected-set objects.
- **Specification:** §§5.2–5.3, 7.1; `ACC-02`, `ACC-03`.
- **Residue:** None.

### `DEC-40` — Immutable run plus child branch, never amend

- **Problem:** Draft `freeze --amend` allowed one run to contain artifacts produced under different authority snapshots.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §4 (`USER-LOCKED`); critique `SOL-004`.
- **Alternatives considered:** mutable manifest versions plus artifact lineage; prohibit reopening; immutable run with cheap branch.
- **Adopted decision:** Any frozen-input change creates a new `run_id` with `parent_run_id`; parent remains untouched.
- **Rationale:** Branching preserves auditability and usability without an event-sourced lineage engine.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Child runs may duplicate/reference prior files and require explicit revalidation.
- **Supersedes:** same-run amendment and manifest-version history.
- **Specification:** §§5.2–5.4; `ACC-03`.
- **Residue:** Copy/link/rebind mechanics are free.

### `DEC-42` — Three orthogonal state axes

- **Problem:** A single delivery enum cannot faithfully represent semantic triage, mechanical progress and render degradation.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §§8, 13 and prompt §7.
- **Alternatives considered:** one overloaded status; proliferated semantic variants; separate axes.
- **Adopted decision:** Keep semantic triage, production/mechanical state and render state independent.
- **Rationale:** A semantically strong package may have a PDF problem; a mechanically valid final may still warrant rerun; an exception is neither.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Derived status has more than one field, but less ambiguity.
- **Supersedes:** draft delivery classes as the sole state and proposed `READY_DOCX_ONLY`-style proliferation.
- **Specification:** §§7.5–7.6; `ACC-05`, `ACC-06`.
- **Residue:** Exact field names are free.

### `DEC-26` — Static staleness, no dependency engine

- **Problem:** Replaced upstream artifacts can invalidate downstream outputs, but a graph engine is disproportionate.
- **Controlling authority:** supported P0.9 synthesis and Directive keep/delete list.
- **Alternatives considered:** automatic dependency graph; rerun everything; fixed hash/invalidation table.
- **Adopted decision:** Fixed per-artifact input hashes and a small static invalidation table report staleness; nothing executes automatically.
- **Rationale:** The dependency set is tiny and stable.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some conservative manual restart may occur.
- **Supersedes:** generalized dependency machinery.
- **Specification:** §§7.2–7.3.
- **Residue:** None beyond implementation shape.

## 4. Semantic-stage and artifact decisions

### `DEC-19` — Three stages retained

- **Problem:** Whether P0.9 needed an additional planner, judge, portal or rerun stage.
- **Controlling authority:** unsuperseded P0.8.0 topology; Directive §5.
- **Alternatives considered:** P0.7 four stages; three stages; extra judge/rerun stage.
- **Adopted decision:** Decision Architecture → Native Package Writer → Elastic Final Writer/Review.
- **Rationale:** It separates nonlinear decision work, native prose production and final reconstruction without adding a quality-theatre stage.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Three cluster chats per cluster remain human-initiated.
- **Supersedes:** P0.7 four-stage topology and any fourth standard stage.
- **Specification:** §6.1.
- **Residue:** `GATE-10`, `EXP-08` map roles to current models.

### `DEC-12` — Human clustering, sequence and priority

- **Problem:** Model-generated planning duplicated a decision the operator can make directly.
- **Controlling authority:** user high-agency workflow, P0.8 cluster control, KISS objective.
- **Alternatives considered:** model cluster plan; automatic similarity clustering; human assignment.
- **Adopted decision:** Human assigns clusters/order/priority; approximately 6–10 jobs per cluster is a starting default.
- **Rationale:** Removes a model stage, schema and prediction confound while preserving useful shared context.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Human performs one bounded planning action.
- **Supersedes:** `cluster_plan.json` and model planner.
- **Specification:** §6.3.
- **Residue:** `EXP-01`.

### `DEC-13` — Delete Priority A/B prediction and differentiated treatment

- **Problem:** Priority labels mixed opportunity prediction, order and writing treatment.
- **Controlling authority:** supported review synthesis; no user decision requiring prediction.
- **Alternatives considered:** preserve labels/treatment; keep prediction only; human priority only.
- **Adopted decision:** Human priority may sort work; every core job receives the same stage topology and quality contract.
- **Rationale:** Removes a confound and avoids weakening supposedly lower-priority packages.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** No automated resource differentiation.
- **Supersedes:** P0.8 Priority A/B treatment.
- **Specification:** §6.3.
- **Residue:** None.

### `DEC-09` — Canonical `strategy.json`

- **Problem:** Need mechanical identity/reference validation without dual truth or brittle Markdown parsing.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §9.1; reopened P0.8 serialization decision.
- **Alternatives considered:** stable-label Markdown; JSON; JSON plus authored Markdown; front matter plus prose.
- **Adopted decision:** JSON is canonical; a generated read-only view is optional.
- **Rationale:** A single structured artifact supports validators and downstream consumers while explicit elasticity prevents it becoming final authority.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Structured fields can encourage boilerplate/anchoring.
- **Supersedes:** P0.8 canonical strategy Markdown and draft dual-format alternatives.
- **Specification:** §9.1.
- **Residue:** `EXP-04` may motivate a later amendment.

### `DEC-10` — Strategy is explicitly non-factual, non-final and elastic

- **Problem:** Structure can make an upstream hypothesis feel contractual.
- **Controlling authority:** final-Sol elasticity and two-axis authority model.
- **Alternatives considered:** implicit prose disclaimer; final-vs-strategy compliance; explicit schema metadata plus no-compliance rule.
- **Adopted decision:** Strategy communicates hypothesis/non-final/elastic status; no downstream rule requires agreement.
- **Rationale:** Mechanical strictness can coexist with semantic freedom only when authority is explicit.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some repeated metadata exists where it prevents material misinterpretation.
- **Supersedes:** any strategy-compliance or deviation-justification framing.
- **Specification:** §§4.4, 9.2.
- **Residue:** None.

### `DEC-33` — Mechanical strategy validation; semantic density stays soft

- **Problem:** Draft hard caps could truncate material requirements and turn P0.7 counts into law.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §9.3–9.5; critiques `SOL-010`–`SOL-012`, `OPUS-003`.
- **Alternatives considered:** hard word/cardinality limits; no structure; structured schema with soft guidance.
- **Adopted decision:** Hard validation covers identity, schema, enums, IDs and true external constraints only; semantic counts/lengths are guidance or warnings.
- **Rationale:** Code should prevent malformed state, not optimize prose by proxy.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some long or dense strategies pass mechanically and require semantic review.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-11`, `≤12 requirements`, 1600-word failure and inherited P0.7 count limits.
- **Specification:** §§9.3–9.4, 14.
- **Residue:** `EXP-04` evaluates format quality, not a hard budget.

### `DEC-23` — Narrow dynamic CV and full replacement

- **Problem:** Tailoring must be material without allowing models to rewrite factual chronology.
- **Controlling authority:** P0.8.0 dynamic-CV design, current user permission, P0.8.1 replacement hardening adopted into P0.9.
- **Alternatives considered:** static CV; model-owned full CV; narrow dynamic zones with append merge; full replacement.
- **Adopted decision:** Headline, profile, competencies and employment bullets are dynamic; chronology remains baseline-owned; present bullet overrides replace fully.
- **Rationale:** Produces real positioning change while preserving formal identity and deterministic merge semantics.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some unusual roles may require a future baseline/source maintenance change between runs.
- **Supersedes:** positional/append merge and model-authored chronology.
- **Specification:** §§11.2, 12.1–12.3.
- **Residue:** None.

### `DEC-24` — Page and length rules are soft unless truly technical

- **Problem:** Historical hard page gates could delete strong content or reject usable documents.
- **Controlling authority:** P0.8.0 soft-page rule; Directive §9.3–9.5; verified sparse-CV imbalance.
- **Alternatives considered:** fixed one/two-page hard gate; typography auto-fit; review-oriented guidance.
- **Adopted decision:** Page/paragraph/bullet counts guide editing; only corruption, unusability or a true external limit hard-fails.
- **Rationale:** Page count is an imperfect proxy for product quality.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Humans may review occasional over-length packages.
- **Supersedes:** P0.7 hard page rules.
- **Specification:** §§12.3–12.5, 16.7.
- **Residue:** `GATE-05`.

### `DEC-25` — Final evidence references follow final content

- **Problem:** Stage 3 may change evidence while stale Stage-1 refs survive.
- **Controlling authority:** P0.8.1 hardening adopted into P0.9; factual-authority boundary.
- **Alternatives considered:** inherit strategy refs; compare to mutable draft; final-stage synchronization.
- **Adopted decision:** Stage 3 updates the flat final factual-ref array to the evidence actually used.
- **Rationale:** Audit provenance must describe the final package, not upstream intention.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Semantic coverage remains a model/human obligation; code cannot prove it.
- **Supersedes:** draft/reference equality and any claim that resolvability proves support.
- **Specification:** §§13.3, 14.4; `ACC-08`.
- **Residue:** `EXP-05` concerns navigation only.

## 5. Evidence and personality decisions

### `DEC-14` — Lean deterministic evidence index in baseline

- **Problem:** Stable provenance/reference validation is needed without a retrieval platform.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §10.1–10.2 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** no stable addresses; manual index; semantic ranking/RAG; generated structural address layer.
- **Adopted decision:** Generate a deterministic, source-linked, non-semantic index over the three factual authorities.
- **Rationale:** One address coordinate supports Stage-1 planning, source navigation, final refs and audit while remaining disposable.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** The generator is additional deterministic code and does not prove semantic completeness.
- **Supersedes:** evidence references based on prose substring matching.
- **Specification:** §§4.1–4.2, 9, 14.4.
- **Residue:** `EXP-05` tests navigation value.

### `DEC-34` — No byte partition; factual and mechanical namespaces stay separate

- **Problem:** Draft index overbuilt exhaustive coverage and included `CV_BASELINE` in the same namespace.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §10.3–10.5; critiques `SOL-008`, `OPUS-007`, `GEM-010`.
- **Alternatives considered:** exact non-overlapping byte partition; heading/ID addresses; factual plus baseline shared namespace.
- **Adopted decision:** Address usable factual evidence without an exhaustive partition; source escape is unconditional; baseline refs are mechanical and separate.
- **Rationale:** The stronger mathematical guarantee had no downstream consumer and created authority leakage.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** A factual passage may require direct source access before a new stable address is added/generated.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-15` and `INV-28` byte-coverage theorem.
- **Specification:** §§4.2, 14.4; `ACC-08`.
- **Residue:** Exact ID syntax is free.

### `DEC-35` — Personality ON, evidence-bound zero-to-three facets

- **Problem:** Whether personality should be absent/off or integrated as an authenticity feature.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §11 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** no personality; OFF optional stub; full-profile injection at every stage; bounded Stage-1 distillation.
- **Adopted decision:** ON by default; Stage 1 selects 0–3 facets after factual grounding; Stage 2 receives distilled guidance; Stage 3 may ignore it.
- **Rationale:** Proper integration can increase specificity while the authority boundary prevents factual inflation.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Adds one interpretive source and a small strategy interface.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-16` OFF default and critique proposals to defer the interface.
- **Specification:** §10; `ACC-09`.
- **Residue:** `EXP-02` tunes value/expression but cannot silently reverse ON.

### `DEC-17` — No auxiliary preparation model in baseline

- **Problem:** Cheap-model preparation could reduce attention but also create anchoring and false exclusion.
- **Controlling authority:** research evidence, KISS objective, no baseline dependency.
- **Alternatives considered:** global cheap-model filter; job-local advisory pass; deterministic index/direct source only.
- **Adopted decision:** No auxiliary model layer in baseline; any pass is a bounded experiment.
- **Rationale:** Cheapness is not value, and false exclusion can remove the best evidence before the strongest model sees it.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Stage 1 bears the full initial evidence-selection workload.
- **Supersedes:** Luna/global preparation as baseline.
- **Specification:** §3; `EXP-03`.
- **Residue:** `EXP-03` only.

## 6. Review, recovery and retry decisions

### `DEC-37` — `RERUN` is triage only

- **Problem:** Draft special RERUN workflow created lifecycle, staleness and history complexity.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §6 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** fourth rerun stage; dedicated Sol rerun contract; triage label plus normal stage restart.
- **Adopted decision:** `RERUN` means broad reconstruction is preferable; the human chooses and starts ordinary stage(s).
- **Rationale:** The label predicts action without creating a subsystem.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Restart-point choice remains human judgment.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-20`, RERUN-specific strategy mutation and special lifecycle rules.
- **Specification:** §§7.5–7.6, 13.5.
- **Residue:** `EXP-06` calibrates triage.

### `DEC-36` — Bounded retry with `[3]` implementation default and manual fallback

- **Problem:** Need visible recovery without fixing an arbitrary attempt count or building orchestration.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §7 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** exact two attempts; hidden automatic retry; no retry; settings-driven manual escalation.
- **Adopted decision:** Retry remains bounded/job-local; if cheap, expose `retry_start_stages`, default `[3]`; otherwise use manual restart and `BLOCKED_MANUAL`.
- **Rationale:** Preserves easy Stage-3 recovery while refusing a workflow engine.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some jobs defer earlier than a fully automated system might recover them.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-18` as a normative two-attempt rule.
- **Specification:** §15; `GATE-11`, `ACC-14`.
- **Residue:** Array values may be tuned without amendment.

## 7. Rendering, date and parseability decisions

### `DEC-21` — Job render transaction; independent document verification

- **Problem:** Converter exit success and batch-level promotion can hide partial document failures.
- **Controlling authority:** P0.7 evidence, P0.8.1 render hardening adopted into P0.9, Directive §13.
- **Alternatives considered:** batch render; job render with one process; per-document process; persistent daemon.
- **Adopted decision:** Job is the transaction; each document result is verified independently; invocation granularity is gated.
- **Rationale:** Limits blast radius and preserves successful outputs.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Per-document conversion may cost startup time.
- **Supersedes:** batch-level success inference and persistent daemon.
- **Specification:** §16.2; `GATE-03`, `GATE-04`.
- **Residue:** Process grouping is implementation evidence.

### `DEC-41` — PDF failure is orthogonal graceful degradation

- **Problem:** Draft simultaneously required four files and permitted DOCX-only fallback.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §13 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** all-four-or-fail; configurable DOCX-only mode; semantic/render separation.
- **Adopted decision:** Both DOCX files are minimum conforming documents; PDFs are normal targets; failed automation is explicit render degradation, not semantic failure.
- **Rationale:** A good package remains usable and manually exportable.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Human PDF export may be required.
- **Supersedes:** draft `CTR-01`/`ACC-17` all-four hard failure and critique demand to remove fallback.
- **Specification:** §§7.5–7.6, 16.1; `ACC-06`, `ACC-16`.
- **Residue:** `GATE-03`.

### `DEC-38` — Document date fixed at semantic finalization/approval

- **Problem:** Run-freeze date may be stale by the time the application is approved; rerender must remain stable.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §14 (`USER-LOCKED`).
- **Alternatives considered:** render-time current date; run-freeze date; finalization/approval date.
- **Adopted decision:** Date becomes canonical at semantic finalization/approval and is preserved on pure rerender.
- **Rationale:** Aligns employer-facing semantics with actual finalization while preventing accidental date drift.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Final package/co-owned metadata needs an explicit date-finalization step.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-22` run-freeze date.
- **Specification:** §16.4; `ACC-17`.
- **Residue:** Exact UI/field location is free.

### `DEC-39` — Parseability outcome, not universal body placement

- **Problem:** Draft mandated body-flow duplication before testing actual masters.
- **Controlling authority:** Directive §15 (`USER-LOCKED`) and direct P0.7 master evidence.
- **Alternatives considered:** header-only by assumption; universal duplication; empirical extraction outcome.
- **Adopted decision:** Critical identity/contact and logical order must extract successfully; move/duplicate only on failed tests.
- **Rationale:** Actual template/parser evidence is stronger than generic ATS folklore.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Step 2 must run parseability tests and may need a template edit.
- **Supersedes:** draft `DEC-29` mandatory body-flow placement.
- **Specification:** §16.5; `GATE-06`, `ACC-18`.
- **Residue:** Placement is implementation evidence.

### `DEC-30` — Fresh repository; selective P0.7 reuse

- **Problem:** Legacy topology contains valuable mechanics and obsolete product/state assumptions.
- **Controlling authority:** user fresh-runtime decision and verified P0.7 evidence.
- **Alternatives considered:** copy P0.7; retrofit P0.8 tree; build fresh and mine proven primitives.
- **Adopted decision:** Fresh repository; inspect, adapt and verify assets by fit.
- **Rationale:** Preserves implementation capital without importing obsolete surfaces.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Some proven code must be re-integrated rather than reused wholesale.
- **Supersedes:** backward-compatibility requirement.
- **Specification:** §§3, 16.3, 22.2.
- **Residue:** `GATE-02`–`GATE-09`.

## 8. Security and human-boundary decision

### `DEC-27` — Control/data separation, local deterministic tooling, no external action

- **Problem:** Untrusted vacancies/model outputs must not alter authority or trigger irreversible actions.
- **Controlling authority:** user no-submit boundary and security research.
- **Alternatives considered:** regex “AI firewall”; broad sandbox platform; small structural boundary.
- **Adopted decision:** Control plane is frozen policy, authority definitions and permissions; candidate/vacancy/personality/model content are protected data plane; local deterministic tooling has no network/model calls or submission capability.
- **Rationale:** Least privilege and human gates reduce blast radius without pretending prompt injection is solved.
- **Accepted tradeoff:** Human performs final submission and portal entry.
- **Supersedes:** autonomous actions and lexical security theatre.
- **Specification:** §17; `ACC-19`.
- **Residue:** `GATE-10` verifies current surface privacy.
