## Part A — Full records for the load-bearing decisions

### `DEC-01` / `DEC-02` — Core is an invariant CV + letter; portal is detached

**Problem.** `RQ-01`. P0.7 and P0.8 carried portal answers inside the core package; P0.8.1 hardened that surface substantially (channel matrix, question identity, limit validation, delivery coupling). The user permitted detachment (`D-011`, `D-012`) without selecting it, and the real vacancy inventory does not exist in the package.

**Authority and evidence.** `[USER-PERMISSION]` for both. `[RESEARCH]` platform behavior is employer-configurable and heterogeneous; a portal often replaces free text with question modules; FINN commonly routes elsewhere; exact limits are frequently not documented globally. `[VERIFIED]` P0.7 accepted duplicate portal question IDs. Estimated portal prevalence ~20%, uncertain.

**Alternatives.** (a) Keep portal in core and implement the P0.8.1 hardening; (b) keep a conditional portal branch, unimplemented, behind a flag; (c) detach entirely and delete the surface.

**Decision.** (c). Core produces one tailored CV and one tailored letter for every core job, unconditionally, in DOCX and PDF. Portal answering is a downstream, human-initiated assist with a thin contract, not required for release. All portal machinery is deleted from schemas, validators, state and metrics. `letter_required` is also deleted: both documents are always produced.

**Rationale.** Portal facts are per-vacancy and employer-configured, so a core model of them is either wrong or maintained by hand — a new manual truth layer, against the objective's second priority. Option (b) is the worst outcome: it pays the schema and reasoning cost of a branch that is never exercised, which is exactly the "contract zombie" the evolution analysis warns about. Deleting the surface also deletes its defects: the duplicate-question-ID class disappears rather than being fixed. Producing an occasionally unused letter is far cheaper than a conditional product.

**Consequences.** Positive: intake, schemas, validation, rendering, status and release criteria all shrink; a whole family of P0.8.1 contracts is retired. Negative: a portal-heavy real batch would push work back onto the human, and the downstream assist has no built tooling on day one. Mitigated by `GATE-07` and the explicit re-adjudication trigger (>20% exceptions or two sharing a reason).

**Confidence.** Medium-high on the product logic; medium on the prevalence assumption, which is why the gate exists rather than a guess.

**Replaces / retains.** Replaces P0.8 §portal handling and every P0.8.1 portal hardening. Retains the principle that exact employer limits are hard constraints — now enforced by the human at capture time, since no core state claims to know them.

**Spec refs.** §3.1, §3.2, §19, `CTR-01`, `CTR-13`. **Dependencies.** `GATE-07` (blocking, human, pre-freeze).

### `DEC-04` / `DEC-03` — `nb-NO` core; everything else is an exception class

**Problem.** `RQ-02`. P0.8 supported Norwegian and English. The user permitted Bokmål-only (`D-013`); the actual language mix is unknown.

**Alternatives.** (a) Keep the language branch; (b) Bokmål-only with English jobs dropped; (c) Bokmål-only core with a first-class `EXCEPTION` class that keeps non-conforming jobs visible and human-handled.

**Decision.** (c). `language` becomes a schema constant. Any job the invariant core cannot serve is marked `EXCEPTION` at intake with a reason, recorded in the manifest, excluded from the core expected set, validation, rendering and metrics, and handled by the human.

**Rationale.** A language branch multiplies prompts, writing standards, phrase guidance and template fixtures for a share of the batch nobody has counted. Dropping jobs silently is unacceptable — the human approved them. The exception class costs one enum and one field and preserves the strongest property of the design: a weak or unusual job never contaminates the strong majority.

**Consequences.** Non-Norwegian jobs receive no automation. If the batch turns out to be materially multilingual, `GATE-07` forces re-adjudication before production, not after.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanism (exception class), medium on `nb-NO`-only surviving contact with the real inventory.

**Spec refs.** §3.3, §3.4, §6.5, §17.6. **Dependencies.** `GATE-07`.

### `DEC-06` / `DEC-07` / `DEC-08` — Job-centric runtime, one frozen manifest, fully derived status

**Problem.** `RQ-03`. P0.7 used physical stage directories, movement scripts and sentinel files; P0.8/P0.8.1 kept a stage-centric tree plus `progress.json`, `context_snapshot.json`, an expected-set object and status files. The user permitted a job-centric fresh runtime (`D-016`, `D-019`) without selecting it.

**Authority and evidence.** `[USER-PERMISSION]`; `[VERIFIED]` P0.7's `_DONE.md` and heading parsing accepted substrings — completion theatre; `[VERIFIED]` Stage 4 could mutate the very draft it was compared against.

**Alternatives.** (a) Inherit the stage-centric tree with P0.8.1 hardening; (b) job-centric tree with a maintained per-job status file; (c) job-centric tree with one frozen manifest and status derived on demand.

**Decision.** (c). One immutable `run_manifest.json` per run; one current artifact per (job, stage); all status, queues and reports computed from manifest expectations, artifact presence, validation results and hashes. No authoritative progress or snapshot file exists.

**Rationale.** Every duplicated status object is a reconciliation problem and a lie waiting to happen. Deriving status is cheap here — a few dozen jobs, a handful of artifact types — and it makes `INV-10` structural rather than aspirational: there is no marker to fake, because nothing reads markers. The job is already the failure, delivery, rerun and rerender unit; making it the directory unit removes the mismatch that motivated the movement script.

**Consequences.** Positive: `advance.ps1` and its two unverified PowerShell tests disappear; the P0.8.1 snapshot/progress/expected-set hardening collapses into one manifest; continuation after an interrupted chat becomes a reconciliation, not a pointer. Negative: status derivation must re-read and re-validate artifacts, which costs a little runtime and requires the validators to be fast and deterministic.

**Confidence.** High.

**Replaces / retains.** Replaces the stage-centric tree, `advance.ps1`, `_DONE`, `progress.json`, `context_snapshot.json`. Retains P0.8.1's strongest ideas — expected set from frozen intake, validator-before-completion — now with a single owner.

**Spec refs.** §7, `CTR-03`, `INV-10`, `INV-21`. **Dependencies.** `GATE-10` (atomic write behavior), `GATE-11`.

### `DEC-09` / `DEC-10` / `DEC-11` — Canonical `strategy.json`, generated human view, tighter density budget

**Problem.** `RQ-04`, described in the package as the most consequential unresolved interface. P0.8 normatively specified `strategy.md` at 900–1400 words. Structure eases validation but risks generic field-filling and upstream authority inflation.

**Authority and evidence.** `[NORMATIVE]` control is Markdown, explicitly reopened by `RQ-04`. `[RESEARCH]` a manual chat response is not schema-guaranteed merely because JSON was requested; Codex-style surfaces can validate locally. `[RESEARCH]` preliminary labels anchor later models. `[VERIFIED]` the runtime already validates JSON artifacts.

**Alternatives.** (a) Stable-label Markdown with a section parser; (b) JSON canonical with a generated Markdown view; (c) JSON plus independently authored Markdown; (d) Markdown body with structured front matter.

**Decision.** (b). `strategy.json` is canonical; `strategy.view.md` is generated, marked do-not-edit, and read by no model stage. Epistemic constants (`STRATEGY_HYPOTHESIS`, `NON_FACTUAL_NON_FINAL`, `ELASTIC`) are mandatory schema fields. Prose budget tightened to 450–900 words soft, 1200 warning, 1600 hard fail.

**Rationale.** The failure modes worth preventing at this interface are mechanical (dropped evidence references, a missing headline hypothesis, an unresolvable reference, a missing requirement map), and structure prevents exactly those. One parser family across four artifacts is materially cheaper to build and test than adding a bespoke Markdown section parser — priority 3 in the objective. The human never reads the canonical form, so Markdown's readability advantage is recoverable by generation. (c) is rejected outright as dual truth; (d) reintroduces the YAML-duplication problem P0.8.1 rejected.

The density cut is a real change: a structured artifact carries its structure in the schema, so the same decision content needs fewer words, and a shorter strategy anchors the writer less.

**Consequences.** Positive: mechanical reference validation becomes possible, which `DEC-25` depends on. Negative and acknowledged: JSON invites field-filling and can make an upstream hypothesis feel like a contract. Countermeasures are explicit — few top-level surfaces, optional fields omitted rather than filled, mandatory non-authority constants, Stage-3 vacancy-first read order, and no downstream instruction to "comply with" the strategy.

**Confidence.** Medium. This is the decision most likely to be reversed by evidence, and it is deliberately cheap to reverse: the semantic contract is frozen independently, so a reversal changes one schema and one prompt.

**Spec refs.** §10, `CTR-05`. **Dependencies.** `GATE-01` (validation ergonomics), `EXP-04` (falsification, after a real baseline).

### `DEC-12` / `DEC-13` — Human clustering and order; delete Priority A/B and differentiated treatment

**Problem.** `RQ-05`. P0.8 carried a model-generated cluster plan with Priority A/B prediction, ordering and differentiated writing treatment. The bounded review noted the prediction/order/treatment confound.

**Alternatives.** (a) Keep model cluster planning and A/B treatment; (b) keep the labels, drop differentiated treatment; (c) human clustering and order, priority as a human sort key only, no model planning, no A/B.

**Decision.** (c).

**Rationale.** A model-generated plan needs a stage, a schema, a freeze, and plan/execution reconciliation — for a decision the human already makes better, with real knowledge of deadlines and personal interest. Predicted priority is confounded with quality: if treatment varies by predicted priority, the pilot can no longer attribute quality differences to anything. Deleting the label removes a state object, a stage, a schema and a confound in one move. Nothing in the package identifies a consumer that needs it.

**Consequences.** Human effort rises by a few minutes per batch (assigning clusters in a spreadsheet). That is the cheapest possible place to spend human time, and it is bounded and one-off per batch.

**Confidence.** High.

**Spec refs.** §6.1, §6.2. **Dependencies.** `EXP-01` (cluster size only, not planning).

### `DEC-14` / `DEC-15` / `DEC-25` — Deterministic evidence index is baseline core

**Problem.** `RQ-06`. The index is an attractive but unimplemented synthesis. Historical retrieval work in the candidate-source lineage found no recall gain from added cross-references and a precision drop, which is a real argument for deferral.

**Alternatives.** (a) Defer to Step 3; (b) adopt as core; (c) adopt a minimal address list without hashes or coverage guarantees.

**Decision.** (b), with a strict non-semantic contract and a lossless-coverage invariant.

**Rationale — and this is the part reviewers should test.** The index is justified by *enforcement*, not retrieval. `DEC-25` requires the final package's evidence references to be synchronized to the final text and mechanically validated. Without stable addresses, that validation degrades to substring matching against prose, which is the same class of check that produced P0.7's completion theatre. Lossless coverage matters for the same reason: if every byte of every source has an address, "no suitable reference exists" is never an excuse for an unreferenced claim. Retrieval value is a separate, unproven question (`EXP-05`) and is explicitly *not* the justification — a negative retrieval result does not remove the index.

**Consequences.** One generator and one schema, regenerable and disposable. Risk: an index tempts later work toward relevance scoring, tiers and claim graphs. Prohibited explicitly in `CTR-14`.

**Confidence.** Medium-high for the enforcement argument; deliberately no confidence claimed for retrieval value.

**Spec refs.** §14.6, §20.2, §20.3, `CTR-14`, `INV-28`.

### `DEC-16` — Personality: optional, minimal facet interface, default OFF for the first batch

**Problem.** `RQ-07`. Only "optional and toggleable" is user-locked (`D-031`); the default and interface are open, and the derived candidate's "default OFF" was flagged as unsupported as a user decision.

**Decision.** Optional and off-safe (inherited). Default **OFF for the first batch**, explicitly interim. Interface: Stage 1 may select 0–3 evidence-bound facets *after* forming the factual picture; each carries desired interpretation, misread risk, prevention rule, supporting references and an expression ceiling; facets travel inside the strategy; **the full profile never leaves Stage 1**; Stage 2 sees only distilled guidance; Stage 3 may suppress everything. Typology vocabulary in employer-facing text is a hard boundary check — a rare justified lexical scan, because the token set is closed.

**Rationale.** The first batch must establish a clean baseline; `Q_happy` has never been measured with real models on real vacancies, so introducing the largest untested semantic variable into the baseline would confound the only measurement that matters. A facet without a supporting evidence reference is a schema error, which is what keeps interpretation from drifting into claims.

**Consequences.** Possible foregone quality in the first batch. Recovered cheaply by a toggle if `EXP-02` is positive.

**Confidence.** High on the interface and boundary; **low-medium on the default** — this is a judgment about sequencing, and a reviewer who prefers ON-by-default with a paired subset is making a reasonable argument.

**Spec refs.** §21. **Dependencies.** `EXP-02`.

### `DEC-18` — Bounded repair: two attempts, then explicit failure and continue

**Problem.** `RQ-09`. Same-session repair is user-permitted (`D-024`); the attempt count was never user-locked.

**Decision.** At most two repair attempts per artifact per stage visit, addressing the reported mechanical defect only. On exhaustion: write a failure record with the invalid artifact and all diagnostics, do not mark complete, continue to the next job. Validator code never triggers a model call.

**Rationale.** One attempt is too few — the common failure classes are one-shot fixes, and the second attempt covers a fix that exposes a second error. Three or more starts to look like semantic churn against a validator, which is where hidden-retry pathologies begin. The count is cheap to change and does not alter any contract.

**Consequences.** Occasional jobs fail mechanically and need human attention. That is the intended, visible behavior.

**Confidence.** Medium-high. Note the honest limit: whether the loop runs in-session at all is `GATE-01`. If it does not, the identical loop is operator-mediated; no architecture changes.

**Spec refs.** §17.1.

### `DEC-19` — Three-stage topology ratified unchanged

**Problem.** Whether to add a standard fourth stage (judge, verifier, portal, planner).

**Decision.** Ratified: `SOL_CLASS` decision architecture → `TERRA_CLASS` package writer → `SOL_CLASS` elastic final writer and review. No fourth standard stage. Roles are specified as capability classes, not product names, with model mapping in settings (`GATE-08`).

**Rationale.** `[NORMATIVE]` and unsuperseded; no user decision changed it; no evidence in the package shows a fourth stage improves packages. Each added standard stage costs a chat per cluster (five per batch), a contract, a validator, a state object and an anchoring surface. A judge stage additionally creates a score proxy — directly against `INV-01` and the selective-quality doctrine.

**Confidence.** High.

**Spec refs.** §8.1.

### `DEC-21` / `DEC-22` / `DEC-29` — Render transaction, frozen document date, identity in body flow

**Problem.** Target-machine rendering is unverified; generic warnings about content controls conflict with direct evidence that P0.7's non-data-bound fill/unwrap method worked; header-only identity is a parseability risk.

**Decision.** Job is the render transaction; every document is independently verified; default posture is one isolated converter invocation **per document** until a target smoke test proves otherwise (`GATE-04`); no daemon. The employer-facing document date is frozen per job at freeze; a pure rerender never changes it. Candidate name and contact must appear at least once in normal body flow in both documents; the header may repeat them.

**Rationale.** `DEC-29` is the interesting one: it deletes a risk class instead of testing and reacting. Moving identity into body flow costs one template edit and makes the header-parsing question a verification detail rather than a design dependency. The date freeze closes a silent-mutation path — re-running a converter must never change what the employer sees.

**Confidence.** High for the transaction and date rules; the per-document invocation posture is explicitly provisional.

**Spec refs.** §18.2–§18.4, §17.4. **Dependencies.** `GATE-02`…`GATE-06`.

## Part D — Where this draft is most attackable

Offered so critics spend their effort where it pays.

1. **`DEC-09` (JSON strategy).** The strongest counter-argument is that a schema makes an upstream hypothesis *feel* contractual to Stage 2 and Stage 3, and that the countermeasures are all `PROTOCOL`, not `BOUNDARY`. If a critic can show that structured strategy measurably suppresses Stage-3 reconstruction, the decision should flip.
2. **`DEC-16` (personality default OFF).** A sequencing judgment, not an evidence-backed one. Reversible either way.
3. **`DEC-01`/`DEC-02` (portal deletion).** Rests on an unmeasured prevalence estimate. The gate is the defense, but a critic could argue the re-adjudication trigger fires too late — after clusters and prompts are already designed.
4. **`DEC-14` (index in core).** The enforcement argument is strong, but a critic could reasonably propose validating references against source headings directly and skipping the index artifact. That is a smaller system; it is also a weaker guarantee.
5. **`DEC-01` invariant letter.** Producing a letter for a job that cannot accept one is deliberate waste. If the real inventory shows many such jobs, the invariance is wrong even though the branch-deletion logic is right.
