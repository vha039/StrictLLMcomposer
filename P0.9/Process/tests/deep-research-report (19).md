## Max Rational Quality report

The strongest pattern is that **Stage 3 medium survives every serious challenge**. This is not cost optimization masquerading as quality optimization. The pipeline already separates substantive reconstruction from a content-locked realization pass, so the writer does not need to “think maximally” about every sentence. OpenAI explicitly warns that higher reasoning effort is not monotonically better, while Anthropic practitioners are reporting exactly the kind of verbosity, self-narration and instruction attenuation that Jobbsøk's voice policy is designed to suppress. citeturn1search6turn20search1turn20search9

The non-obvious finding is therefore that **Max Rational Quality ≠ max/max**. Max or xhigh is rational mainly on Stage 1, where no prose escapes and extra search can uncover claim-boundary mistakes. Even there, Astra-high outranks Astra-max because Stage 1 is highly structured rather than open-ended. This is a genuine interaction effect: the same extra reasoning that plausibly improves the forensic stage can lower total-package quality when moved downstream into the writer.

The strongest case **against rank #1** is Claude Fable 5.1. Independent current aggregate evidence often puts Fable 5.1 ahead of Astra in general intelligence, and Fable is explicitly designed for long-horizon document-heavy work; Astra is only days old, so practitioner evidence is much thinner. citeturn19search2turn22search0turn22search2 I nevertheless leave Astra-high→Astra-medium at #1, narrowly, because Stage 1 needs template/schema discipline as much as raw reasoning and Stage 3 specifically benefits from avoiding the current Claude-family verbosity signal. The gap to ranks #2–#7 is too small to treat as meaningful without pilot data.

The **single missing measurement most capable of overturning this table** is a blind within-vacancy Stage-3 effort sweep—medium versus high/xhigh—scored separately for factual corrections and restrained Bokmål authenticity. My targeted search found no contemporary model comparison measuring this exact Norwegian register. **Overall confidence: moderate.** Model existence, effort controls, prices and privacy posture are reasonably well verified; the A/B/H ordering remains an informed prior.

## Balanced / Daily Driver report

The dominant pattern is **downstream capability concentration**. Ranks #1, #2, #5, #6 and #7 all spend less on Stage 1 than Stage 3. That only makes sense because Jobbsøk's strategy is explicitly non-authoritative: Stage 3 can reject it, reconstruct the case and reopen primary evidence. In a conventional planner→writer chain where the writer receives only the planner's summary, I would not recommend Luna or Gemini as aggressively upstream. Here, information asymmetry and override rights create real fault containment.

The surprising result is **Gemini 3.8 Flash high → Sol medium at #1**, not Sol→Sol. Gemini's current price/performance frontier is unusual: independent testing places it on the cost/intelligence Pareto frontier with very high output throughput, while its structured-output capability maps directly onto Stage 1. citeturn14search6turn4search0 This does **not** mean Gemini is judged the superior writer; quite the opposite. The ranking exploits specialization.

The strongest case **against #1** is subtle cross-vendor anchoring. Stage 1 chooses what to foreground. Even when Stage 3 is formally free to override, a plausible but suboptimal Gemini strategy could narrow the search path enough that Sol never discovers the better thesis. Same-family Terra→Terra or Sol→Sol also has more mature operational evidence than a Gemini→OpenAI pairing. I keep #1 because the handoff is schema-bounded and Stage 3 retains source access, but the 0.02–0.13 point gap across the top seven is effectively a tie before pilot data.

The **most ranking-changing missing datum** is per-configuration **first-pass success probability**: schema clean on first attempt, factual correction count, and manual-rescue incidence. Because each job has a deliberately tiny retry economy, a five- or ten-point difference in clean-completion rate can dominate token prices.

**Overall confidence: moderate-low to moderate.** Price, context and effort controls are solid. The specialization thesis is structurally strong. The unresolved uncertainty is whether Stage 3 truly corrects cheap-planner omissions as reliably as its prompt contract permits.

## Best Budget Option report

The strongest pattern is the **planner/writer capability ratchet**: cheap models appear disproportionately often in Stage 1, but never because I assume their mistakes are harmless. They appear because Jobbsøk has deliberately made Stage 1 non-authoritative. That architecture converts cheaper Stage-1 reasoning from a direct truth risk into a search-quality risk that the final writer can, at least in principle, repair.

The surprising result is that **Gemini 3.8 Flash → Gemini 3.8 Flash wins the literal weighted budget score**, even though it is not my most conservative budget recommendation. Its current promotional rate, structured-output support and independent speed/capability results are unusually strong. citeturn4search0turn14search6 For a real rollout, however, **I would personally choose rank #3, Luna-high → Terra-medium, until the first Bokmål pilot says otherwise.** The difference between 8.76 and 8.70 is entirely within epistemic noise, whereas Terra carries the stronger Stage-3 truth/style prior.

The strongest case **against #1** is therefore precisely its Stage 3. B=7.6 and A=7.9 are not measured facts; they are threshold-clearing priors. There is no current Scandinavian-business-prose benchmark I found that demonstrates Gemini 3.8 medium can preserve finely calibrated claim ceilings while simultaneously sounding natively restrained in Bokmål. If even a small pilot shows more invented connective tissue, formulaic endings, self-grading, or factual corrections than Terra, #1 falls immediately. The challenge does **not** change the mathematical rank today, but it changes my production choice.

The **single missing datum with the highest economic value** is not another public benchmark. It is Jobbsøk's own joint distribution of **Stage-3 output/reasoning tokens, retry probability, and human correction minutes**. At these token prices, manual intervention can easily be more economically important than model tokens, yet that term is currently unobserved.

**Overall confidence: moderate on relative token economics, low-to-moderate on the final ranking.** The budget frontier is clear; which point on it minimizes *human-adjusted* cost is not.
