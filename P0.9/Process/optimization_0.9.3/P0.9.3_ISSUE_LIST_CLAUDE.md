# P0.9.3 final plans — issue list

Compiled 2026-09-06 against `P0.9.3_FINAL_SEMANTIC_PLAN.md` (727 lines) and `P0.9.3_FINAL_OPERATIONAL_PLAN.md` (619 lines). Every claim below is verified against disk.

**Clarified optimization goal governing this list:** maximize expected final output quality. Minimizing credit expenditure is a sub-goal *beneath* that condition, within reason. Items are selected by expected effect on output quality, with a minor downward correction for expected implementation cost. Volume is bounded to roughly 10% of the plans.

**Origin note.** Issues 2, 3 and 5 trace to an error in `P0.9.3_CONSOLIDATED_FINAL_INPUT.md §2.2`, which stated *"Stage 1 reads the digests and ranks; Stage 3 fetches by id and verifies."* That contradicted the user's stated design and was carried into the plans in good faith. The user's position, stated twice in the answered work package: *"stage 3 should [consume full context files] only for specific inquiries … that is the primary function of step 1"* and *"this should only ever be consumed raw by stage 1."* Stage 1 is the stage that reads.

## Primary list — the critical items

### I-1 · Stage 1 is told to write a field that does not exist, and to drop one that does · **BLOCKING**

**Evidence.** `P0.9.3_FINAL_SEMANTIC_PLAN.md:516`, inside the complete replacement Stage-1 instruction:

> Populate all six expression fields: plain_language_label, intended_employer_interpretation, **expression_mode**, expression_ceiling, expression_guardrail and misread_risk.

`strategy.schema.json` `personality_facets.items` sets `additionalProperties: false` and allows exactly: `facet_id`, `plain_language_label`, `evidence_refs`, `expression_ceiling`, `expression_guardrail`, `vacancy_relevance`, `intended_employer_interpretation`, `misread_risk`. **`expression_mode` exists nowhere in the repository.** `cli.py:1044-1056` forwards six keys downstream, including `vacancy_relevance`.

**Reasoning.** Two failures. Every Stage-1 submission that selects a facet is mechanically rejected on an unknown key, forcing the bounded-recovery ladder on every job. If hand-corrected, `vacancy_relevance` is permanently `null` in `personality_distilled`, silently removing one sixth of the personality route the P0.9.2 pass was built to deliver. Every other reference in both plans says "six fields", and the operational plan preserves `cli.py`'s existing six-key computation — so this is an isolated transcription slip, not a decision.

**Recommended fix.** Replace `expression_mode` with `vacancy_relevance` at that line. Verify the slip appears nowhere else. One word.

### I-2 · Stage 1 is provisioned as a retrieval consumer, not as the stage that reads · **CRITICAL**

**Evidence.** `P0.9.3_FINAL_OPERATIONAL_PLAN.md §4.2` replaces *"the default full-authority/full-index reading interface"* with: `read.evidence_ids`, `read.evidence_fetch`, `read.authority_digests`, `read.mandatory_source_refs`, and `optional_source_access` marked *"on-demand, not a read-everything list."* The Stage-1 instruction at `SEMANTIC:456-465` directs it to *"Read authority_digests and fetch mandatory_source_refs"* and to fetch records used in `evidence_plan`.

Stage 1's default working surface is therefore roughly 25 digest rows, an id list, and the ceiling sections — until it chooses to fetch.

**Reasoning.** Stage 1's entire function is evidence selection and proof architecture: deciding which of ten project records, four education records and the identity/positioning material best reduces *this* employer's uncertainty. That decision is made against a summary table whose "Can prove" and "Hardest ceiling" columns are agent-authored prose. The plan contradicts itself here — the same instruction says *"Digests, route advice and strategy prose are not substitutes for sources"* while making the digest the default selection surface.

This is the credit sub-goal overriding the quality objective at precisely the stage where quality is decided. A weaker selection at Stage 1 cannot be repaired downstream: Stage 3 inherits the architecture, and the evidence Stage 1 never considered is evidence no one considers.

**Recommended fix — Codex decides between two options, under quality-first:**

- **(a) Full context to Stage 1.** Stage 1 receives the three authorities in full, as today. Fetch remains available for Stage 3 and for precision re-reads. Highest quality confidence, highest Stage-1 token cost (~45k), zero new failure modes.
- **(b) A sufficient representation plus discretionary fetch.** Stage 1 receives a materially richer default surface than the current digest — a hyper-compressed, keyword-dense projection carrying enough of each record's actual content to make an *informed* judgment about what to open, then fetches full entries at its own discretion with no budget or discouragement. This is only acceptable if the representation is genuinely sufficient to rank on; a five-column summary is not.

Either way, the framing *"on-demand, not a read-everything list"* must go for Stage 1. Stage 2's removal and Stage 3's selective reading are unaffected — those were the user's actual instruction.

### I-3 · Stage 1 has no source for the baseline references it is required to write · **CRITICAL**

**Evidence.** `strategy.schema.json` requires `cv_direction.employment_focus` entries matching `^BL:(EMP|EDU|SEC)_[A-Z0-9_]+$`, and `DEC-34` validates that each resolves in the mechanical baseline map. The new bundle table (`OPERATIONAL:201`) lists `read.baseline_map` without stage qualification, and the Stage-1 instruction (`SEMANTIC:456-465`) never mentions baseline at all. Current `cli.py` supplies `baseline_map` only for `stage >= 2`.

**Reasoning.** Stage 1 must emit values it has no supported way to discover. This is not hypothetical: it produced a hard submission rejection in the `r20260905-005939-3eeb10` Stage-1 run, recovered only by reading `generated/baseline_map.json` directly — a path the new design explicitly discourages. Under fetch-first provisioning Stage 1 has *less* access than when that failure occurred, so the failure becomes systematic rather than incidental.

**Recommended fix.** Supply Stage 1 with the mechanical baseline projection already specified for Stage 3, or at minimum the `employment_id` / `education_id` / `section_id` inventory with their `BL:` forms. Resolves automatically under I-2 option (a). Codex owns the bundle design and should state which it chose.

### I-4 · The Stage-3 substantive-pass permission is silently dropped · **CRITICAL**

**Evidence.** The current live `stage3_final_writer_review.md:63-66` contains:

> **A realization-only pass follows.** You do not have to buy smoothness here by deleting signal. Preserve the strongest evidence and judgment, and let the polish pass make it read well. When a passage is both important and awkward, keep it and fix it below — do not resolve the tension by cutting it.

That text sits inside the `## Sequence` region. `SEMANTIC:535` instructs: *"Replace its body through the section immediately preceding 'Final polish — content lock' with the text below."* The replacement sequence's step 8 says only: *"Then apply the unchanged Final polish content lock."* Grep confirms no equivalent survives anywhere in either final plan.

**Reasoning.** This paragraph is not decoration; it is the mechanism that makes the content lock work. The P0.9.2 design rested on a specific finding: the polish pass's value is not that it finishes the text, but that separating substance from register *removes the substantive pass's need to buy smoothness with signal* — and that the permission must be written **where the substantive pass reads it, or it changes nothing.** Without it, the substantive author still resolves the "important but awkward" tension by cutting, and the lock inherits an already-thinned package. This reintroduces the exact deletion pressure that removed `EV:WORK#025` in three consecutive runs.

The plan preserves the lock block verbatim — correctly — but the lock protects content it will never receive.

**Recommended fix.** Carry the paragraph into the new Stage-3 sequence, immediately before step 8. Wording may be compressed; the two load-bearing elements are *a realization pass follows* and *do not resolve important-but-awkward by cutting*.

### I-5 · Full-source permission exists in the architecture but not where the model reads it · **HIGH**

**Evidence.** The amendment text (both plans) states *"Original authority access remains unconditional."* The old Stage-1 instruction said it directly: *"You may always open the original authority file directly … The index is a navigation aid, not a boundary."* The new Stage-1 instruction contains no such sentence; the nearest is *"Use evidence_ids and evidence_fetch to inspect candidates and alternatives."*

**Reasoning.** Identical failure pattern to I-4: a permission placed in a document the stage does not read. A model reading only its frozen instruction will treat fetch as the sanctioned interface and will not open a full authority when the situation warrants it — which is precisely when quality depends on it. Architecture permission that never reaches the operator is not permission.

**Recommended fix.** Restore an explicit sentence in the Stage-1 and Stage-3 instructions granting unrestricted original-source access. Largely subsumed if I-2 resolves to option (a), but the sentence should exist either way.

### I-6 · The AI-differentiator amendment may not produce the requested behaviour change · **HIGH**

**Evidence.** Register row `C63-N06; FLAG-03` disposes: *"Consider AI-enabled delivery when concrete evidence helps this employer's decision, even without 'AI' in the ad; **no automatic private-sector/low-regulation rule.**"* The replacement Norwegian text at `SEMANTIC:276-282` says selection follows *"oppgaven og bevisverdien, ikke automatisk etter sektor."*

The user's stated request: *"I want it to appear more often as a differentiator … particularly in vacancies that are in the private sector, are less strictly regulated than health for instance and/or is more technical/innovation oriented."*

**Reasoning.** Codex's reasoning is defensible — sector is a proxy, and an evidence-value test is more principled. But the previous rule was *already* an evidence-value test (`Other §4.1`: subordinate unless AI is decisive), and it produced the under-use the user is objecting to. Replacing one evidence-value test with another may change nothing observable. The user asked for a frequency change and named the conditions; the plan removed the conditions without flagging that it was overriding a stated preference.

This is a quality question, not a compliance one: the AI evidence is now anchored by a registered three-year designation and current ML delivery work, so under-use is a real cost.

**Recommended fix.** Either restore the vacancy-property conditions as *permissive triggers* (not automatic selection), or keep the evidence-value framing and add an explicit statement that AI-enabled delivery is a legitimate differentiator in its own right rather than a fallback — and surface the change as a user decision rather than a silent override.

### I-7 · The digest becomes the de facto ranking surface, unmitigated · **HIGH**

**Evidence.** `SEMANTIC:401-411` defines one maintained table per authority with columns `Record | Source section | Lifecycle | Can prove | Hardest ceiling`, agent-maintained from source paragraphs. `OPERATIONAL:196` projects them into `read.authority_digests`. The Stage-1 instruction requires fetching records *"used in evidence_plan or a material requirement"* — i.e. fetch happens **after** selection.

**Reasoning.** Ranking therefore occurs against agent-authored summaries; only the winners are ever read in full. That inverts the intended safeguard. It also concentrates risk in a single hand-maintained artifact with no sync mechanism: after the confirmed factual updates, a stale "Can prove" row silently mis-ranks a record for every future vacancy. And "Hardest ceiling", singular, understates records carrying several live boundaries.

**Recommended fix.** State in the Stage-1 instruction that digests are navigation only and are never a basis for a selection decision or a citation; require the candidate set to be read in full before ranking, not merely before committing. Under I-2 option (a) this largely dissolves. Under option (b), it is the essential guard and must be explicit.

## Secondary list — real, lower value

- **S-1 · Digest staleness has no owner.** The digests must track the confirmed factual updates (`PRJ-03` → in use 2026/2027, `PRJ-06` → completed and handed over). The plan specifies the initial update but no rule keeping them synchronized afterwards. Recommend one line: any authority factual edit requires the corresponding digest row be updated in the same pass.
- **S-2 · "Hardest ceiling" is singular.** Several records carry multiple live boundaries. Mitigated by mandatory `W §10` refs and fetch-before-use; worth a plural column or an explicit "not exhaustive" note.
- **S-3 · The nested branch namespace is the plan's lowest quality-ROI item.** Seven schema regex changes, a resolver, flattened generated-path keys and staging-outside-ancestor logic, for an organisational benefit with no output-quality return. It is a user-requested feature and correctly designed; noted only because under the clarified priority it is the one substantial mechanical cost that buys no quality. `--from-stage` alone delivers the redo capability.
